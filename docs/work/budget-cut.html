Budget decisions

Protecting results through a double-digit budget cut

When budgets took a double-digit cut at a medical device division, I wrote the media mix models that showed senior leadership where to protect investment. Leads fell by only a fraction of the decline we assumed based on past cuts.

The situation

A cut that size can be spread evenly or placed deliberately, and the division needed to know what each choice would cost. This is a long-consideration purchase, with more than a year between a patient's first lead and a completed procedure, so waiting to see what the cut did to procedures wasn't an option.

What I did

  • Wrote the Python media mix models myself, using a holdout and regression modeling so the guidance rested on incremental effects and not on correlations.
  • Worked with Finance on where to protect spend.
  • Took the results to the executive team: the division President and the VPs of Marketing, Sales, and Finance.
Result: leads fell by only a fraction of the decline we assumed based on past cuts.

Demo: the same approach on made-up data

The real data stays private, so I rebuilt the method on synthetic data. It simulates three years of weekly leads for a made-up brand across five channels, with carryover (adstock) and diminishing returns (saturation). Everything from here down is simulated. That has one advantage: I can grade the model against the truth, which you never get to do with real data. It's also a best case, since the data follows the model's own equations and the forecast below uses actual holdout spend. Real data won't be this kind.

Demo: two ways to take a 20% cut

One simulated world can flatter a method, so I reran the whole exercise on 30 of them. Reallocating beat the even cut in truth every time. But the model overstated the gain in 93% of runs, promising 2.1 points on average and delivering 1.2. That's the optimizer's curse: it moves money toward wherever the estimates happen to run high. The 95% ranges held up for the even cut, covering the truth 97% of the time, but were too narrow for the reallocation (70%) and the gain (63%). So I trust the direction of a reallocation, and I'd discount the size of the promised gain before taking it to Finance.

Demo: what the model got wrong

Total media leads came in at 711 a week against a true 850, and channel estimates were noisier than the total. Email is the clearest miss. The model put its saturation point at about 20K a week against a true 7K, believed the channel had room to grow, and gave it the largest increase the guardrails allow. That's the kind of move I'd validate with a geo test before committing to it.

The code

The two transformations at the center of the model, from the repo:

def adstock(x, decay):
    #carryover: this week's effective spend includes a decayed share of last week's
    out = np.zeros_like(x, dtype=float)
    carry = 0.0
    for i, value in enumerate(x):
        carry = value + decay * carry
        out[i] = carry
    return out

def hill(x, half_sat, shape):
    #diminishing returns: 0 at no spend, 0.5 at half_sat, approaches 1
    x = np.maximum(x, 1e-9)
    return x**shape / (x**shape + half_sat**shape)
