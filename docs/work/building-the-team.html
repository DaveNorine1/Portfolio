Building the team

I took over a small operational support team at a medical device division and built it to more than three times its size, into a regular voice in budget and strategy decisions. It's the third analytics practice I've built.

What changed

When I arrivedNow
TeamA small support groupMore than three times the size, with employees and contractors across data science, analytics, BI, and analytics engineering
RoleOperational supportA regular voice in budget and strategy decisions with the executive team
InfluenceWork that informs an eight-figure annual budget in patient and physician marketing

How I built it

  • Hired for the foundation. I brought on an analytics engineer and a data steward.
  • Set the standards behind every readout. UTM and data quality rules, core reporting automated in Snowflake and dbt, Tableau dashboards for media and email journeys, and a Streamlit app for attribution and UTM governance.
  • Connected marketing to outcomes. I set up ROI tracking from lead to completed procedure, with lead-to-patient matching in a PHI-protected environment. A holdout group would have cost too much, so we used causal inference modeling to estimate the effect. Shifting spend to what worked produced a double-digit lift in completed procedures.
  • Developed people. I pushed the analysts to use AI every day for SQL and Python, and two analysts with no data science background moved into ETL and machine learning work. I also built a library of custom GPTs the team uses.
  • Partnered across functions. IT, Finance, Sales, and Field Marketing on matched-market tests, Legal and IT on consent, Legal, Privacy, and IT on the AI agent, and Compliance and ad-promo review on content language.
