Leading through change

Rebuilding measurement after losing most of our tracking

A cookie consent change wiped out most of our trackable traffic at a medical device division. I rebuilt the KPI framework around what we could still trust and, with Legal and IT, helped drive the changes that recovered a large share of it in early results.

The situation

The dashboards kept running after the change, but they now described only the visitors who had opted in. Leadership needed to know which numbers still meant what they used to, and we needed a way to get the data back without crossing any privacy lines.

What I did

  • Rebuilt the KPI framework and ran a KPI workshop, so we could keep making decisions while the data was incomplete.
  • Worked with Legal and IT and helped drive state-by-state opt-out consent and server-side collection.
  • Kept person-level data to measurement only. It's never used for targeting or advertising.
Result: early results show a large share of the lost traffic trackable again.

How I think about what to trust

This is the general version of the framework, with the company specifics taken out. When tracking gets partial, each question should move to the source least affected by the loss.

The questionRead this firstBe careful with
Is the business healthy?First-party outcomes in the CRM, such as leads and completed proceduresSite-analytics conversion counts, which fell with consent
Did marketing work?Geo tests and media mix models, which use aggregates and don't need user-level trackingPlatform-reported conversions and last-touch attribution
Is this page or campaign improving?Rates within the consented group, compared over time and against the same periodComparing raw volumes across the date of the change
Is the data itself okay?Share of traffic that's trackable, reported as its own KPIAssuming consented visitors look like everyone else

Limits of the early read

These are early results. And a higher trackable share doesn't make the sample representative, because the people who can be tracked may still differ from those who can't. That's why the framework stays in place after recovery.
