Forecasting consumer sentiment

For my University of Michigan capstone, Paul Stotts, Ali Alrubaiee, and I forecast the Consumer Sentiment Index from more than 40 monthly FRED series. Our best-looking score was an R² of 0.989. The number I'd lead with is about 0.68.

What I worked on

I co-developed the Python modeling and analysis: lagged features, changes, rolling statistics, and spreads, followed by regularized regression and tree-based models with time-aware evaluation.

Why the best score doesn't hold up

A forecast that good should make you suspicious. Two things in our own outputs explain it:

  • A three-month moving average of sentiment itself carried 94% of feature importance. The model was mostly predicting sentiment from recent sentiment.
  • The scenarios barely moved the forecast, and a mild recession nudged sentiment up by 0.5%, which makes no economic sense. A model that responded to the economy would move more, and in the other direction.

The team separately reported a held-out LASSO R² of about 0.68 under a different protocol. The two numbers aren't directly comparable, and I'd lead with the lower one. If I picked this back up, the first thing I'd add is a naive baseline that just carries sentiment forward, so that every model has to beat it.

What the correlations show

That's association, and it doesn't show cause. It's still useful for a planner deciding which indicators to watch.
